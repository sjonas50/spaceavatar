## Why Mars
- Mars is the most Earth-like world we can reach: it has seasons, polar ice caps, ancient river valleys, and a 24.6-hour day — a Martian day is called a "sol."
- It's small (about half Earth's diameter), cold (average around −60°C), and its air is thin carbon dioxide — about 1% of Earth's surface pressure — so liquid water can't last on the surface today.
- Billions of years ago Mars was warmer and wetter, with lakes and maybe seas. Finding out where that water went — and whether anything ever lived there — is the driving question of Mars exploration.
- Mars looks red because its soil is full of iron oxide — the planet is literally rusty.

## The rover dynasty
- Sojourner (1997) was the first rover on Mars — the size of a microwave oven, it drove about 100 meters total and proved roving was possible.
- Twin rovers Spirit and Opportunity (2004) were designed for 90-sol missions; Spirit lasted six years and Opportunity kept driving for nearly fifteen, covering a marathon-plus 45 km.
- Curiosity (landing 2012) is car-sized and nuclear-powered; in Gale Crater it confirmed an ancient freshwater lake environment that could have supported microbial life. Still operating as of mid-2026.
- Perseverance (landing 2021) explores Jezero Crater, an ancient river delta, and is caching rock samples in sealed tubes for a future return to Earth. Still operating as of mid-2026.
- The "seven minutes of terror": a Mars landing happens faster than radio signals can reach Earth, so the spacecraft must land itself — Curiosity and Perseverance descended on a rocket-powered "sky crane" that lowered them on cables.

## Ingenuity — the Mars helicopter
- Ingenuity, a 1.8 kg helicopter carried by Perseverance, made the first powered flight on another planet in April 2021 — in air so thin its blades spun about 2,400 times a minute.
- Planned for 5 flights, it made 72 over nearly three years before a rotor was damaged in January 2024 — one of the great overachievers in space history.
- Its first-flight moment carried a tribute: a postage-stamp-sized piece of fabric from the Wright brothers' 1903 Flyer was taped under its solar panel.

## Water and the search for life
- Spacecraft have found water ice at the poles, frozen underground across much of the planet, and minerals that only form in liquid water.
- Curiosity found organic molecules — carbon-based building blocks — in ancient mudstone, and seasonal methane whiffs in the atmosphere that scientists still debate.
- Nobody has found life on Mars. The honest answer is "not yet known" — which is exactly why Perseverance's samples are being cached for laboratories on Earth.
- Mars Sample Return — the plan to fetch those cached tubes — has been redesigned for cost several times and its architecture was still being settled as of mid-2026; the samples wait patiently in Jezero either way.

## Humans to Mars
- A round trip is the hard part: with current rockets it's roughly six to nine months each way, plus waiting at Mars for the planets to align for the ride home — a multi-year expedition.
- Challenges stack up: radiation without Earth's magnetic shield, muscle and bone loss in low gravity, growing food, and landing very heavy cargo through that thin atmosphere.
- NASA's approach treats the Moon as the proving ground — Artemis technologies and Gateway experience feed the Mars plan. SpaceX develops Starship with Mars settlement as its stated long-term goal.
