## NASA — and how it began
- NASA was created in 1958, spurred by Sputnik, absorbing the earlier NACA aeronautics agency. It is a civilian agency — a deliberate choice to keep exploration public and scientific.
- Its centers each have personalities: Johnson (Houston) trains astronauts and runs mission control, Kennedy (Florida) launches, JPL (California) flies the robot explorers, Goddard runs space telescopes.
- "Houston" was the first word spoken from the Moon's surface because mission control sits in Houston — capsule communicators, flight directors, and hundreds of specialists behind every flight.

## The international cast
- ESA, the European Space Agency, pools more than 20 countries: it flew Rosetta to a comet, builds the Orion spacecraft's service module, and launched the Euclid dark-universe telescope.
- JAXA (Japan) is the sample-return specialist: Hayabusa and Hayabusa2 brought back the first asteroid samples; its SLIM lander pulled off a pinpoint Moon landing in 2024.
- ISRO (India) reached Mars orbit on its first try (2014) and in 2023 landed Chandrayaan-3 near the lunar south pole — the first nation to land in that scientifically prized region.
- CNSA (China) has landed on the Moon's far side (Chang'e 4, a world first), returned lunar samples — including the first far-side samples with Chang'e 6 in 2024 — landed the Zhurong rover on Mars, and operates its own Tiangong space station.
- The International Space Station is the biggest peacetime engineering collaboration in history: NASA, Russia's Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA, and Canada (whose robotic Canadarm2 is a national icon) have kept it crewed continuously since November 2000.

## The commercial space revolution
- SpaceX made rockets reusable — Falcon 9 boosters land and refly routinely, which cut launch costs dramatically — and its Dragon capsule has carried NASA astronauts since 2020, ending a nine-year gap in American crew launches.
- Starship, SpaceX's fully reusable super-heavy rocket, kept flight-testing through the mid-2020s; a version of it is NASA's contracted Artemis lunar lander.
- Blue Origin flies tourists past the edge of space on New Shepard and debuted its heavy-lift New Glenn rocket in January 2025; its Blue Moon lander is contracted for later Artemis missions.
- Rocket Lab's small Electron rocket launches from New Zealand and once reached orbit with a battery-powered pump-fed engine — proof there's more than one way to build a rocket.
- Commercial cargo and crew turned NASA into a customer rather than a sole operator — a model now extending to space stations and lunar landers.

## Space is for everyone
- More than 600 people from over 40 countries have flown to space. The Artemis program aims to land the first woman and first person of color on the Moon.
- Private astronauts, teachers, artists, and researchers now reach orbit; the age range of space travelers spans from 18 to 90 (Star Trek's William Shatner flew at 90).
- The Outer Space Treaty of 1967 — signed at the height of the Cold War — declares space the province of all humankind: no nation can claim the Moon.
