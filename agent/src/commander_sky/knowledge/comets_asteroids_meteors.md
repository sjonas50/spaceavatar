## Sorting the space rocks
- Asteroid: a rocky body orbiting the Sun, most between Mars and Jupiter. Comet: an icy body that grows a glowing tail near the Sun. Meteoroid: a small fragment in space.
- A meteor is the streak of light when a fragment burns up in the atmosphere — a "shooting star" is no star at all. A meteorite is the piece that survives to the ground.
- Most visible meteors are caused by fragments the size of a grain of rice — they glow because they slam into air at tens of kilometers per second.

## The asteroid belt and beyond
- The main belt between Mars and Jupiter holds millions of asteroids — but Hollywood lies: they're so spread out that spacecraft fly through without dodging, and all of them together weigh far less than our Moon.
- Ceres, the largest belt object at about 940 km across, counts as a dwarf planet and has bright salt deposits hinting at briny water below the surface.
- Beyond Neptune lies the Kuiper Belt — icy worlds including Pluto — and far beyond that the theorized Oort Cloud, a vast shell of dormant comets reaching a good fraction of the way to the next star.
- Short-period comets like Halley's (returning 2061) come from the Kuiper Belt region; comets that visit once in thousands of years fall in from the Oort Cloud.

## Comets up close
- A comet is often described as a "dirty snowball" — ice, dust, and rock a few kilometers across. Near the Sun it sprouts two tails: a curved dust tail and a straight blue ion tail, both pointing away from the Sun, not behind the comet.
- ESA's Rosetta orbited comet 67P for two years and landed the Philae probe on it in 2014 — the first comet landing, on a duck-shaped world of cliffs and boulders.
- Meteor showers are comet crumbs: Earth crossing a comet's dusty trail gives us the Perseids every August (comet Swift-Tuttle) and the Geminids every December.

## The dinosaur connection
- About 66 million years ago an asteroid roughly 10 km wide struck near today's Yucatán Peninsula, leaving the 180-km Chicxulub crater and triggering the extinction of the non-bird dinosaurs.
- The giveaway was a thin worldwide clay layer rich in iridium — an element rare on Earth but common in asteroids — found before the crater itself was identified.
- Impacts that size are once-in-tens-of-millions-of-years events; sky surveys have found the overwhelming majority of dinosaur-killer-class asteroids near Earth, and none found is on a collision course.

## Planetary defense — we've actually practiced
- In 2022 NASA's DART spacecraft deliberately rammed the small asteroid moon Dimorphos and shortened its orbit by about half an hour — the first real test of nudging an asteroid, and it worked better than predicted.
- ESA's follow-up mission Hera launched in 2024 to survey the crash site up close and turn the experiment into an engineering manual (arriving late 2026).
- OSIRIS-REx brought back about 120 grams of asteroid Bennu in 2023 — pristine rubble containing water-bearing minerals and organic molecules, the kind of ingredients impacts may have delivered to early Earth.
- The strategy for a real threat is early detection plus a small nudge decades ahead — which is why telescopes that hunt near-Earth asteroids matter more than any last-minute heroics.
