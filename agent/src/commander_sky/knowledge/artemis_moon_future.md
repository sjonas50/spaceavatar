## Artemis — going back to stay
- Artemis is NASA's program to return humans to the Moon — named for Apollo's twin sister in Greek myth. The goal isn't flags and footprints again: it's a sustained presence.
- Artemis I (2022) flew the uncrewed Orion capsule around the Moon and back, stress-testing the heat shield at 40,000 km/h reentry.
- **Artemis II flew in April 2026**: astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen launched April 1, swung around the far side of the Moon on April 6, and splashed down in the Pacific on April 10 — the first crew at the Moon since 1972. They also set the record for the farthest humans have ever traveled from Earth.
- Christina Koch and Victor Glover are set to be, respectively, the first woman and first person of color to fly to the Moon's vicinity — a deliberate statement about who exploration belongs to now.
- Artemis III is planned as the landing mission — the first bootprints since Apollo 17 in 1972 — using a version of SpaceX's Starship as the lander, targeted for later this decade (schedules have moved before; treat dates as "planned, not promised").

## The hardware
- SLS (Space Launch System) is NASA's Moon rocket — taller than the Statue of Liberty and more powerful at liftoff than the Saturn V.
- Orion is the crew capsule, with a European-built service module providing power and propulsion — Europe's hardware literally pushes American astronauts around the Moon.
- Gateway is a planned small space station in lunar orbit — a staging post where crews can dock before descending, built with international and commercial partners.
- New spacesuits for the lunar south pole are being built commercially (Axiom Space) — more flexible than Apollo suits and sized for a wider range of astronauts.

## Why the lunar south pole
- Apollo landed near the equator; Artemis targets the south pole, where permanently shadowed craters — places sunlight hasn't touched in billions of years — hold water ice.
- Water ice is the game-changer: drinking water, breathable oxygen, and — split into hydrogen and oxygen — rocket fuel. Mining it means not hauling everything from Earth.
- Some polar crater rims get near-constant sunlight, ideal for solar power — a spot with power next door to a spot with water is prime lunar real estate.
- India's Chandrayaan-3 landing in 2023 and a wave of robotic landers since — including the first commercial landings (Intuitive Machines, Firefly's Blue Ghost) — have made the south pole the busiest neighborhood on the Moon.

## The Moon as a stepping stone
- The Moon is three days away; Mars is many months. Every life-support system, habitat, and rover that fails on the Moon can be fixed — or rescued — from Earth. That's the point of practicing there.
- Gateway and long lunar stays teach deep-space operations: radiation exposure, partial gravity, and living off local resources — the exact skills a Mars expedition needs.
- The stated sequence, as of mid-2026: Artemis landings and a sustained lunar presence through the late 2020s and 2030s, informing a crewed Mars mission after that. Ambitious timelines shift; the direction of travel hasn't.
